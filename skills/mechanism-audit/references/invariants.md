# Mechanical invariants

- External spur gears: parallel axes; pitch-plane center distance rA+rB; compatible module 2r/N; positive overlap of tooth-face intervals; opposite signed tangential speeds. Internal gears use |rA−rB| and same tangential direction. Transform local angular velocity onto a common axis before comparing. These formulas do not apply directly to bevel, helical, worm, or rack meshes.
- Tooth phase: compare actual tooth flanks through a complete mesh period, including profile and backlash. A correct pitch distance does not prove that teeth interleave. Do not treat teeth-count phase offsets as arbitrary decoration.
- Compound wheels: wheel and pinion share the same physical shaft and angular displacement. Coaxial hands may share a center yet rotate independently; never test them as a rigid joint. Check bore/shaft radii and axial spans, not only center coincidence.
- Springs: identify both anchors and the reaction constraint. For a watch barrel, distinguish the held arbor from the rotating drum, and a fixed outer bridle from a slipping automatic bridle. The spring endpoint must reach the shown anchor throughout winding/unwinding. Geometry may be schematic, but anchor roles cannot swap.
- One-way clutch: compare relative input/output speed, not just absolute clockwise labels. Input reversal swaps lock/overrun states while downstream output retains its required direction. An overrunning gear may rotate while its shaft or stem stays still. Show the released coupling, not an impossible rigid connection.
- Intermittent drive: output motion must coincide with a pushing/contact interval. A daily wheel that turns only 0.25° during a demo cannot visibly push a full date pitch unless an explicit snap mechanism stores and releases energy. Test phase continuity at midnight, one exact index per cycle, dwell, and no reverse indexing during return. Detents must contact the indexed wheel and withdraw while a tooth passes.
- Reset cams: test several initial angles and both flanks. Hammer contact should rotate the cam toward its unique zero, then hold. Do not animate a reset count independently of the cam/hand shaft.
- Shared time: gear, hand, escapement, balance and hairspring phases derive from the same simulation time. Speed changes integrate time rather than multiplying all past time. Pause/resume preserves phase. Integer beat counts and continuous oscillator phase need an explicit relationship.
- View consistency: normalize installed and inspection transforms to a shared model frame. Compare IDs, centers, axes, working planes, gear radii, contact positions, phase and visibility at the same state. Copy position, quaternion AND scale when a shaft/finger changes size. Any intentional view-only cutaway or spacing offset must be named and cannot be used to claim real contact.

# Useful failure probes

Introduce a known gap, move a wheel to the wrong axial layer, invert one gear speed, detach a hand from its shaft, let the oscillator use wall time while the train pauses, or omit a changing child scale from the inspection clone. The relevant regression must reject each injected fault. For the current watch, also check day 31→1 and automatic winding with a stationary crown.
